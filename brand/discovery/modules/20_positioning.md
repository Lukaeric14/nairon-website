# Module 20 — Positioning

Participant: Luka (CEO). Date: 2026-07-13.

## Raw

**Desired word-of-mouth sentence (category frame):** "They build AI employees" — "They built us an AI employee that does our permits. It's real work, not a chatbot." Category defined by the work owned, not the relationship, the control layer, or the platform. (Offered and not chosen: "our AI department," "AI you can trust," "the humans+AI workspace.")

**Competitive alternatives displaced (multi-select + verbatim addition):** the next hire (another coordinator/dispatcher/admin — "we compete with a salary"); vertical SaaS promises (ServiceTitan/AppFolio automation tiers that underdeliver); and verbatim: "ai engineer perhaps" — the in-house AI engineer hire.

**Only-we claim:** "Both arms, one company" — consultants who own the outcome + a workspace platform built in-house for supervised agents. Agencies rent infrastructure; SaaS won't send humans.

**Price posture:** "Accessible premium" — premium look and feel, priced to win the land grab; the brand signals quality, not exclusivity; volume over margin right now.

## Synthesis

**Positioning statement (three candidate formulations, Dunford/Moore form):**
1. For operators in Solar, HVAC, and Property Management who are about to solve an operations gap with another hire, an AI engineer, or another SaaS tier — Nairon builds AI employees that own the job and prove it with a number. Unlike AI agencies or software vendors, Nairon is both the consultants who deploy the work and the platform (Hive) it runs on.
2. Nairon builds AI employees for operating businesses: real jobs, measured outcomes, supervised in one workspace — delivered by the same company that built the platform.
3. The AI employer's firm: we find the job, build the employee, run it under supervision on our own platform, and sign our name to the outcome.

**Category:** "AI employees" (work-owned framing). Anti-categories to avoid in copy: chatbot, copilot, automation tool, AI assistant.

**Competitive frame for the site:** price and proof anchored against a salary and against an AI engineer's salary; tone anchored against SaaS feature promises (their weakness: promises without humans; our claim: humans who own the outcome).

**Design implication (binding for Phase 1):** accessible premium — adopt the premium mechanics (restraint, editorial type, proof blocks) but keep entry signals open: clear CTA, plain pricing language when it exists, no exclusivity theater ("by invitation," gated everything). Legora's "gorgeous brochure that whispers instead of closing" criticism is the specific failure to avoid.

**Open questions:**
- The anti-model (competitor admired but not to be copied) not yet named — ask in personality module.
- Whether "AI employees" as a term needs defending against skepticism/hype-fatigue in copy — test in voice module.
