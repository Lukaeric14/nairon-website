# Module 30 — Audience & Niche

Participant: Luka (CEO). Date: 2026-07-13.

## Raw

**Sweet-spot client (verbatim):** "Mid-sized, 50-150. Making at least $3M/yr. Ideal $10M/yr. Ideally someone without a very lengthy procurement process, multiple decision makers, no buyer committees."

**The buyer (verbatim):** "Ops leader with budget approved. Site speaks to the person with the problem and budget. Could also be owner."

**Anti-ICP (multi-select + verbatim additions):** procurement mazes (enterprise RFP/security-review cycles); automation bargain hunters ($500 Zapier jobs, price shoppers); "anyone comparing us to offshore engineers"; "someone wanting custom software outside of the AI employees scope."

**Dominant objection the site must answer:** "This is too advanced for us" — we're a roofing company, not Google. Required answer: no new software, runs inside your systems, we do the setup. (Offered and not chosen: "AI will embarrass me," "I got burned before," "my team will reject it" — these remain secondary objections to address, not the lead.)

## Synthesis

**ICP card:** Solar / HVAC / Property Management operator, 50–150 employees, $3M–$10M+ revenue, ops leader (or owner) with problem + budget + authority in one person. Deal shape must close without committees.

**What the anti-ICP list implies for the brand:**
- "Not comparable to offshore engineers" → never sell hours, effort, or headcount; sell owned jobs and outcomes. Pricing language must be per-outcome or per-engagement, never per-developer.
- "No custom software outside AI-employee scope" → the offer must look productized: named roles (Permit Agent, Maintenance Coordinator), defined pilots, standard artifacts.
- Bargain hunters repelled by the premium register — working as intended; procurement mazes repelled by the fast, committee-free engagement framing.

**The intimidation finding (binding for design and copy):** the #1 objection is capability-anxiety, not risk or price. Consequences:
- Copy at plain readability; trade vocabulary, not tech vocabulary; every feature framed as "we do this part."
- The warm temperature (people, real workplaces) must dominate the cool temperature (UI) in imagery balance.
- "No new software, no migrations" (already in docs) is a primary message, not a footnote.
- The Legora move that transfers directly: flattery of the buyer's competence ("exceptional lawyers" → the equivalent for operators), never of the technology.

**Three named segments for the site's vertical pages:**
1. Solar: operations/permitting leaders — proof anchor: $125 → $25 per permit.
2. Property management: portfolio operators — proof anchor: 150 → 250 doors per manager.
3. HVAC: service operators — no proof anchor yet; page leads with department-level analogies until a deployment lands.

**Open questions:** whether the owner-read and ops-leader-read need different page paths or one shared register (current answer: one register, person-with-problem-and-budget).
