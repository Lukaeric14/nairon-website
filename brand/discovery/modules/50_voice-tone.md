# Module 50 — Voice & Tone

Participant: Luka (CEO). Date: 2026-07-13.

## Raw

**Hard bans, all confirmed:** (1) No hype vocabulary — revolutionary, cutting-edge, supercharge, unleash, game-changing, 10x. (2) No "bot/chatbot/copilot" — the category is "AI employee"; competitor vocabulary never appears, even to attack it. (3) No exclamation marks. (4) No unexplained tech terms — LLM, agentic, RAG, orchestration banned unless immediately explained in trade language.

**Verbatim addition:** "We need to feel and communicate like Legora. Like a lawyer."

## Synthesis

**Voice doctrine:** lawyer-grade communication for operators. Properties of lawyer speech that transfer: every claim carries its evidence; qualifiers are exact ("closing engagements," not "trusted by the industry"); short declarative sentences; no enthusiasm markers — the facts carry the weight; vocabulary is precise but defined for the listener.

**Combined with Module 40's energy requirement:** the excitement lives in WHAT is said (the era, the numbers, the speed of deployment), never in HOW it's decorated. "AI is the new internet" energy through evidence: "$125 permit, now $25" is an exciting sentence with zero hype words.

**Working rules for all copy:**
- Numbers make claims; adjectives don't.
- "AI employee" is the only category term. Say "the work," "the job," "owns," "runs."
- Trade language over tech language: work orders, permits, doors, dispatch — not workflows, orchestration, integrations.
- Flatter the buyer's ambition, not the technology's power.
- Existing doc phrases confirmed on-voice: "AI employees own outcomes, not steps"; "controlled autonomy, not blind autonomy"; "no new software, no migrations."
- Tone floor from Module 30: nothing may trigger "this is too advanced for us" — every mechanism sentence pairs with a "we do this part" sentence.

**Three candidate voice descriptors:** 1. A lawyer explaining your best option. 2. Precise, current, unhurried. 3. Evidence with momentum.
