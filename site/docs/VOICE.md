# Nairon academy voice

Every lesson article is a rewrite of two people talking. Mahan Salehi (CMO)
asks; Luka Eric (CEO) explains. The article is not a transcript and it is not a
third-party blog post about the topic. It is Luka explaining the thing, in
writing, to a business owner.

The patterns below are lifted from the actual transcripts. Follow them because
they are how these two people talk, not because they are good writing rules.

## What Luka actually does

**Reaches for one concrete analogy, then drops it.** Tokens are "the alphabet
of an LLM". Not a paragraph of metaphor. One image, then straight to mechanics.

**Gives a real number within two sentences of a claim.** From the token lesson:
one token is about four characters, 100 tokens is about 75 words, a 280-character
tweet is about 70 tokens, a ten-page document is nearly 7,000. He never says
"relatively small" when he can say the number.

**Uses a worked example with real content in it.** Not "consider a sentence" but
the actual sentence: "Customers prefer subscriptions and emoji over one-time
purchases", then the actual split — `un-believ-able` is three tokens because
dropping `un` changes the meaning.

**Frames by contrast, in one line.** "A chatbot responds whereas an agent decides
and acts." One clause each side, no build-up.

**Grounds everything in a job someone does.** Go through my emails and reply to
them. Book the job. Chase the invoice. Never an abstract "use case".

**Cross-references the rest of the track.** "We talked about what tool calls
are, what memory is." Assumes the reader is working through a series.

**States things flatly.** No "it's important to note", no "arguably". If he is
unsure he says the model does not know, which is different from hedging.

## What Mahan does

Sets up the question the reader has, in the reader's words: "tokens are the
currency of AI, correct?", "AI agents is a term that gets thrown out a lot".
In the article this becomes the opening problem, not a quoted question.

## Turning the two-hander into prose

The article is written in Luka's register, in the second person, addressed to
one reader. Do not write "In this video Mahan asks Luka…". Do not stage a
dialogue. Take the substance of the exchange and say it directly.

Keep a verbatim line only when it earns a blockquote — a phrasing sharper than
anything you would write. Attribute it with the speaker and the timestamp.

## The audience

Owners and operators of solar, HVAC, property-management, logistics and
staffing businesses. They run crews, chase invoices, and answer phones after
hours. They are smart and busy, and they have no CS background.

- Examples come from their world. A photo of a roof, an after-hours intake call,
  a lease renewal, a $2,000 approval threshold. Never "a SaaS onboarding flow".
- Never explain what a database is. Do explain what a vector database is.
- The reader's question behind every lesson is "do I need to care about this,
  and what do I do differently if I do".

## Register

Plain, direct, mildly dry. Confident without selling. British-neutral spelling
matches the rest of the site (`organisation`, `recognise`).

Write like someone who has built the thing and is explaining it to a peer over
coffee, not like someone documenting it for posterity.

## Hard nos

- No "In this lesson we will…". The heading already said it.
- No selling Nairon inside the article body. The magnet CTA does that job and it
  is rendered separately.
- No invented numbers, customers or outcomes. Every figure comes from the
  transcript. If the video does not give one, the article does not either.
- No summary section at the end. End on the last real point.
