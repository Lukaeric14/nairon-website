Claude Design is Claude's feature for making media assets. You give it a prompt,
it asks a few setup questions, and minutes later a one pager, deck or wireframe
comes back built in your own brand colours. Claude shipped it a couple of weeks
before we recorded this, and people are already calling it the Canva killer.

## What can Claude Design make?

- Slide decks
- One pagers
- Proposals
- Audits
- Pitch decks
- Wireframes, so designs of actual websites
- Marketing visuals
- Infographics
- Interactive prototypes

Anything that ends up on social media, on a website, or on the flyer you leave
behind after a service call.

Photography and drawn illustration are the two things outside it. Everything
here is assembled from code, and code renders shapes and text. The photo of the
finished install is still a photo somebody has to take.

## Claude Design vs Claude Code vs Cowork

[Claude Code](/academy/claude-code) writes code for you.
[Claude Cowork](/academy/claude-cowork) does work for you. Claude Design makes
the things you put in front of other people.

| | Claude Code | Claude Cowork | Claude Design |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Points at | Your codebase | The files on your computer | Your brand kit and design system |
| What comes out | Written and tested code | The job done, like a drafted email or a summary | A finished asset: a one pager, a deck, a wireframe |
| Built for | Someone writing software | Someone running the business | Whoever has to send the thing |

## How do you get Claude Design?

Design is not a separate download. It is one of the surfaces inside Claude
Cowork, sitting alongside projects, artifacts, scheduled tasks and dispatch, so
getting Design means getting the Cowork desktop app.

Which subscription tier includes it is the one thing this lesson will not tell
you. Anthropic's own pricing page is the only place that answer stays current,
and a number typed into an article ages badly.

## What are design tokens, and why does the output not look AI-generated?

The standard complaint about AI output is that it looks like AI output, and a
proposal that looks generated is worse than one you typed yourself.

Claude Design starts somewhere else. Before you generate anything you set up a
design system from your brand kit, and what it pulls out of that kit are your
design tokens: a colour value, a typeface, a corner radius, a spacing step, held
in a form the model reuses on every later asset.

The design system is the seed. Every asset after it is generated from the same
tokens, which is why the proposal you send on Monday and the flyer you print on
Friday look like the same company made them.

## Claude Design vs image generation

Image generation hands you a picture of a design. One PNG, flattened, however it
happened to come out. Claude Design writes code and renders it: layers of code,
shapes, text and elements assembled into a page. You watch the code arrive while
the one pager fills in next to it.

Anyone who has sat through a basic HTML class can read the result. Our one pager
had an H1 at the top, a paragraph tag under it and three columns below that.

## What do you get at the end?

A rendered page you can point at element by element. The H1 is an H1, the three
columns are three columns, so you can name the part that is wrong instead of
describing where it sits in a picture. Ours finished while we were still talking
about it, off a single prompt, following our colours and our design system
closely enough that we kept it.

What we did not test: exporting it, handing it to a designer, editing it after
the fact, or re-running the same asset against a different brand kit. The demo
was one prompt and one finished asset, so treat everything past that as an open
question rather than a promise.

## Claude Design vs Canva: is it a replacement?

| | Canva | Claude Design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | An editable design file you export | Rendered code, in shapes, text and elements |
| Brand consistency | You apply the brand settings and templates yourself | Tokens pulled from your brand kit at generation time |
| Photography and illustration | Yes | No |
| How you make it | Drag and drop on a canvas | One prompt, plus the setup questions it asks back |

The jobs that move across are the text-and-shapes ones where the main
requirement is looking like your company: the one pager, the proposal, the pitch
deck, the wireframe, the infographic. You describe the asset instead of laying it
out, and the brand consistency arrives with it rather than being your job to
enforce.

The jobs that stay in Canva are the ones built around an image. A photograph, a
drawn illustration, a graphic somebody has to nudge by hand until it looks
right. Code cannot render any of those, and the manual canvas is still the place
you drag the thing three pixels left.

Our demo does not settle the killer question either way. One prompt, one one
pager, output good enough to keep, which is a single asset made well and a long
way short of a like-for-like test.

## How to use Claude Design

1. Set the design system up first, from your brand kit and brand colours. Every
   asset after it inherits those tokens.
2. Prompt for the asset. Ours was "create a one pager for how to use Claude
   Design".
3. Answer the setup questions it asks back, then hit continue.
4. Wait while the code renders into the finished asset. Ours took a couple of
   minutes.

Test it on something real. The move-in packet your leasing agents hand over
at the end of a tour, or the pitch deck for a commercial management contract
that nobody in the office wants to build. Run it once and judge the result against
what you would otherwise have sent, not against an agency.
