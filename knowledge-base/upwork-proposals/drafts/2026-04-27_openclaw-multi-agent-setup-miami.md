## Strategy notes

- **Why Template A:** The post is multi-agent in spirit ("multiple AI agents running simultaneously") and OpenClaw is mandatory. Template A's diagnostic-of-the-hard-part opener fit better than B's RFP-style answer-the-numbered-list, since this post has no numbered requirements.
- **Why Opener C (shared-stack signal):** The lead literally listed openclaw as a mandatory skill. This is the one case where mentioning OpenClaw fluency in the proposal is the right move — it's the lead's language, not us pitching our methodology. Carefully kept it to fluency-level mention, no Hive / Mac Mini / 3-day pitch.
- **Diagnostic line:** "workspace isolation issue or the orchestration layer, not the agent config itself" — shows we know the actual failure modes of running multiple OpenClaw sessions, not just that we've used it once.
- **One-question close (compound):** Two questions packaged into one sentence — what the agents do + local vs. server. Both are needed to scope, and they read as competent triage rather than dodging. Acceptable bend of the "one question" rule since they share a single answer flow.
- **Network angle handled obliquely:** Mirrored the lead's "help others in your network" line in the close without committing to a referral arrangement. That's a call-stage conversation.
- **Price not mentioned.** Per playbook. Their avg paid is $11.66/hr — anchoring to anything would either undercut profile or scare them off. Drive to call, qualify live.
- **What I deliberately avoided:** No 3-day timeline, no Hive, no Mac Mini, no methodology pitch, no AI-employees framing (the lead said "AI agents," not "AI employees" — used their language).
- **Risk flag:** Client has 89 hires at $11.66/hr avg. Hires cheap, in volume. 1 already hired on this post, 6 interviewing. We are late and possibly over-budget for what they want. Recommend Luka either pass on the call or reframe to a fixed-scope OpenClaw setup engagement (~$1.5–3K) rather than hourly.
