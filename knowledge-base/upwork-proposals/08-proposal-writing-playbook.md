# 08 — Proposal Writing Playbook

The master file for how the agent drafts Upwork proposals. Read this plus the job post and `10-proposal-templates.md` before writing.

## THE ONE RULE (read this first)

**The proposal is not a pitch. The proposal's only job is to book a 15-minute call.**

Upwork leads receive 40+ proposals. They are actively screening to reject. The fastest way to get rejected is to pitch our own methodology, timelines, stack, or "AI employees" framing before they've decided we're competent.

So in the proposal:

- **Do not** explain what Nairon does.
- **Do not** describe our 3-day timeline, hardware, Hive, OpenClaw, or "AI employees" positioning.
- **Do not** lecture them on what they should want.
- **Do** mirror their stated requirements back cleanly.
- **Do** show — with one specific proof point — that we've shipped work like theirs.
- **Do** answer any numbered/specific asks in the post exactly.
- **Do** end on a sharp, low-friction ask for a short call.

Once we're on the call, Luka can reshape the conversation to fit what we actually do. If the lead ultimately insists on something outside our business model, we pass — but that's a call-stage filter, not a proposal-stage filter.

## Why this matters

Most "AI experts" on Upwork lose the bid by over-pitching. Luka wins by looking calm, senior, and aligned with what the lead already wrote. The proposal proves *fit + competence*, nothing more. The call proves *value + approach*.

## Required structure (every proposal)

1. **Line 1:** Specific reference to the lead's post in their language. Prove you read it.
2. **Line 2–4:** Mirror their problem/requirements back in cleaner form, with one sentence of useful diagnostic shape.
3. **Middle (short):** One past project or credential that maps directly to their use case. One only — don't list a catalog.
4. **If the post has numbered requirements (list 2–3 past automations, give an overrated/underrated take, etc.):** Answer every one, in order, short and specific.
5. **Close:** A specific call ask with the calendar link.
6. **Signature:** "— Luka"

## Length

- **Default: 150–250 words.** Shorter than most competitors' proposals, on purpose.
- **Complex RFP-style posts (numbered requirements): up to 400 words.** Still scannable.
- **Never exceed 450 words.**

## The hook: opener formulas that work

Pick the one that best matches the post. Avoid "Hi! I came across your post…"

### A — Reflect their framing back
> "You're looking for a builder who can ship, not an advisory hire — that's the mode I default to."

### B — Diagnose one thing
> "The piece most teams underestimate in multi-agent content pipelines isn't the writer — it's the QA/editor and the memory layer."

### C — Shared-stack signal
> "I've shipped Claude-based agents against Appfolio + MS365 stacks before — the integration pattern you're describing is one I've built twice."

### D — Direct, calm answer
> "Short version: yes, this is squarely what I do. A few notes on how I'd approach it, plus answers to your requirements below."

## What to include in the body

Pick **two** of the following — not all of them:

- **One past project** that maps to their use case. One only. Be specific about outcome.
- **One sharp opinion** on a detail of what they described (shows senior thinking).
- **A targeted clarifying question** — one, not three. More than one looks like dodging.
- **A direct "yes, I can do that"** — confident and calm beats a long brag.

## What NOT to include

- Our methodology (Identify → Audit → Implement → Optimize). **Kill this from proposals.**
- The 3-day timeline. (Save for the call.)
- "AI employees" framing. (The lead may not use that language. Use *their* language.)
- Hive, hardware, dedicated Mac Minis. (Call-stage material.)
- OpenClaw. (Call-stage material, and too niche to assume they know it.)
- A catalog of skills / services.
- Rate or price (unless they explicitly demand it — and even then, keep it to one line).
- Anything that sounds like a brochure.

## Handling posts with specific proposal requirements

Many of the jobs we target (see `12-target-jobs.md`) require specific things — "list 2–3 past automations with outcomes," "one sentence on what's overrated," "one sentence on what's underrated," "no proposals with fewer than 3 pieces of information will be reviewed."

**Rule: answer every numbered requirement exactly and in order.** If we can't, we're not a fit.

**Overrated takes (usable, real):**
- "Multi-agent frameworks (CrewAI, AutoGen) for most real-world jobs — one well-instrumented agent with solid tool use and memory usually beats a swarm."
- "Fine-tuning — most problems dissolve with better prompting, retrieval, and workflow design."

**Underrated takes (usable, real):**
- "Observability and replay — you can't tune what you can't see."
- "Human approval gates — they're what separates a prototype from a production system."
- "Boring ops work — retries, cost caps, logging — more than fancy orchestration."

## The close (CTA)

Always end on a specific, low-friction ask. Rank in order of preference:

1. **Direct ask for the call:** "Worth 15 minutes to talk through it? https://cal.com/mahan-javaheri-aswf9u/15min"
2. **Ask them to confirm fit:** "If this looks aligned, happy to jump on a short call — grab a slot here: https://cal.com/mahan-javaheri-aswf9u/15min"
3. **Offer to send examples:** "Happy to DM two past examples if useful."

**Never leave the close generic** ("Let me know if interested!"). That's the kiss of death on Upwork.

## Voice checklist (run before sending)

- [ ] First person ("I")
- [ ] No "hope this finds you well" / "I am writing to express my interest"
- [ ] No sentence longer than 25 words
- [ ] Not a single sentence about Nairon's methodology, 3-day timeline, Hive, hardware, or OpenClaw
- [ ] No emojis (unless the lead used one)
- [ ] Signs off "— Luka"
- [ ] One specific proof point, not a list
- [ ] Clear call-to-action with the calendar link
- [ ] Under 250 words (or under 400 if the post has multi-part requirements)

## Red-line review before sending

The agent re-reads and asks:

1. **Would a senior founder who's reading 40 proposals spend 20 seconds on this?** If not, shorten.
2. **Did I pitch Nairon or did I mirror the lead?** If I pitched, rewrite.
3. **Is there one genuine detail specific to this lead?** If not, rewrite.
4. **Does the CTA give them exactly one easy next step?** If not, fix it.

## The call is where we sell

Once the call is booked, Luka and the agent handle it from there. On the call we can:
- Reframe what they asked for into what we actually deliver (AI employees, 3-day v1, ongoing).
- Float the "first AI employee free" qualifier if they're clearly ICP.
- Ask the discovery questions in `11-qualification-discovery.md`.
- Pass if they insist on something off-model.

**The proposal is the door. The call is where the work happens.**
