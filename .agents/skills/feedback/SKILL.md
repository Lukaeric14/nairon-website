# Feedback

Use this skill to help a person give useful feedback while the moment is still fresh.

## Core Rule

Deliver feedback as close to real time as practical: same day, ideally within an hour.

Fresh feedback feels like calibration. Stored feedback feels like a case file. Keep delivery to 60-90 seconds, use a 1:1 setting, and prefer voice or live conversation over email.

## The Flow

Use three moves in order.

1. Ask the question:
   - "How did this go compared to your expectations?"
   - Listen first. Often they already saw it and own the fix.
   - If their read matches yours, affirm it, confirm next step, stop.

2. Share the observation only if needed:
   - "When you [observable behavior]..."
   - "I experienced [your reaction]..."
   - "That resulted in [business or relationship impact]..."
   - Then stop. Do not pile on, hedge, or explain the motive.

3. Confirm it landed:
   - "Does that land with you, or did you see it differently?"
   - "What's the key feedback you're taking away?"
   - "What next step are you considering?"

If their takeaway misses the point, restate once and ask what surprised them. If it still does not land, pause and revisit later.

## Hard Constraints

- Never say "because you" or attribute motive.
- State what a camera or transcript could verify.
- Own your reaction instead of making it universal truth.
- Connect to a concrete team, project, customer, business, or relationship impact.
- Give one piece of feedback at a time.
- Avoid hedges like "this is not a big deal" or "do not take this wrong."
- Do not save it for the next scheduled 1:1 unless timing or safety requires it.
- Give praise with the same specificity as criticism.

## Openers

Use these when there is no clean question to lead with:

- "Got 60 seconds? I want to share something I noticed."
- "Can I give you a quick observation while it is fresh?"
- "One thing from that meeting I want to flag."

## Praise Version

Same structure, positive impact:

```text
When you [specific behavior], I experienced [positive reaction].
That resulted in [concrete useful impact].
Keep doing that.
```

Catch people doing the useful thing if you want them to repeat it.

## Prep Prompts

### 1. Clean the Behavior

```text
Here is what I want to give feedback on: [raw notes or transcript].

Pull out only observable behavior. What did the person actually do or say?
Strip my interpretations, motive guesses, and character judgments.
List any "because you" assumptions I made and mark them banned.
Return one clean sentence I can use as the "When you" line.
```

### 2. Tailor to the Person

```text
About to give feedback to [name].

Here is what I know about how they work: [profile, prior 1:1 notes, review, preferences].
Here is the cleaned behavior and impact: [paste].

Tell me:
1. How this person likely receives feedback.
2. Which step will be hardest for them to hear, and what to watch for.
3. Three likely answers to "How did this go compared to your expectations?"
For each answer, tell me whether to stop at Step 1 or move to Step 2.
```

### 3. Rehearse

```text
Role-play as [name] using the profile above.

I will deliver this feedback: [opener + Step 1 question + three-sentence observation + Step 3 check].
Respond as [name] would, including likely defense, deflection, or "yes but" moves.
Then step out of role and tell me one line in my delivery that made deflection easier,
plus a tighter alternative.
```

## Debrief Prompts

Run these soon after the conversation.

### 4. Grade Delivery

```text
Here is the transcript, recording summary, or notes from a feedback conversation: [paste].

Grade my delivery against the Feedback skill:
1. Did I lead with the question or skip to the observation?
2. Did I stay in observable behavior or attribute motive?
3. Did I stack multiple feedback points?
4. Did I hedge?
5. Did I run the takeaway check, and did their takeaway match?

Score each 1-3. Quote the exact line where I nailed or missed it.
End with one move to change next time.
```

### 5. Update the Person Profile

```text
Based on the graded conversation, update my profile on [name].

What new pattern showed up in how they received feedback?
What words or framing landed?
What made them defensive?
What should I watch for next time?

Output additions or edits to the existing profile, not a new doc.
```

## Common Failure Modes

- Skipping the first question.
- Adding motive or character judgment.
- Stacking several issues into one conversation.
- Hedging until the feedback sounds bigger and less honest.
- Waiting too long.
- Giving criticism often and praise rarely.
- Delivering sensitive feedback in writing when a short live conversation would work.
